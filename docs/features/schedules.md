# Schedules

- **Status:** My week and the department week are built and styled for the midterm and show real shifts from the shifts API (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#38); edit mode is built (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#16); on laptops the department week is a week calendar with a department picker (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#82, slice CAL2 of workforce-ops-app/.github#114; it replaced the timeline of workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#53, decision 0038), with a people menu on its left (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#83, slice CAL3)
- **Related:** workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#3; backend: [schedules and shifts](https://github.com/workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-backend/blob/main/docs/features/schedules.md)

## In short
Employees see their upcoming shifts and their department's week; managers plan the week, fill open shifts, and cancel or reassign shifts. Times are always shown in the workplace's time zone. Kept short on purpose; details are added when the screens are built.

## Server requests used

Through `js/api/shifts.js`:

| Action | Endpoint |
|---|---|
| List shifts for a week or date range | `GET /api/shifts?from=&to=&department_id=` (one department's week), or `&mine=true` (your own shifts) |
| One shift | `GET /api/shifts/{id}` |
| Add / change | `POST /api/shifts`, `PATCH /api/shifts/{id}` |
| Assign, or make open | `POST /api/shifts/{id}/assign` |
| Cancel | `POST /api/shifts/{id}/cancel` |
| Several at once | `POST /api/shifts/batch` with `confirm_count` when 10 or more shifts change |
| The department week's heading (the company's active departments, `id` and `name` only, for anyone signed in) | `GET /api/departments` (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-backend#88) |
| The department's people, for "Unscheduled employees" | `GET /api/users?department_id=`, asked only when the session includes `user.view`; a 403, or a 404 while the backend's people endpoints do not exist yet (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-backend#54), leaves the section out and the rest of the week still shows |
| The home screen's time off: your own requests, and how many wait for your review | `GET /api/time-off-requests?mine=true` (with `time_off.request_self`), `GET /api/time-off-requests/waiting` (with `time_off.review`; the same request as the menu's count, sent once per page load), through `js/api/time-off.js` ([time off](time-off.md)) |
| The home screen's open work: this week's shifts in your own department | `GET /api/shifts?from=&to=&department_id=` for the current week (with `schedule.edit` and a home department) |
| Your own department, which the department week shows by default | no extra request: the session every signed-in page already loads (`currentSession().user.department_id`, [session and navigation](../architecture/session-and-navigation.md)) |

## The API answer the screens expect

Agreed for the shifts API (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-backend#61), following [0026](https://github.com/workforce-ops-app/.github/blob/main/docs/decisions/0026-api-conventions.md):

```
GET /api/shifts?from=YYYY-MM-DD&to=YYYY-MM-DD[&department_id=...][&mine=true][&cursor=...]
{ "items": [Shift, ...], "next_cursor": "..." | null }
```

Each shift: `id`, `department` (`id`, `name`), `employee` (`id`, `display_name`, and `roles`: the person's active roles in the shift's department as `{name, built_in}`, `built_in` being `owner`, `administrator`, `manager`, `employee`, or `null` for a role the company added (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-backend#159); or `null` for an open shift), `starts_at` and `ends_at` (UTC), `timezone`, `status`, `details`, `notes`, `event_name`, `event_description`. The department and employee names are included so the week can be shown without separate requests for departments and people, which arrive after the midterm. `mine=true` lists the signed-in person's own shifts, as time off does.

`js/api/shifts.js` asks the server every time and follows `next_cursor` until the last page, for shifts and departments alike. There is no built-in sample data: the pages show only what the server returns, so made-up shifts can never appear in a real deployment. To see a populated week locally, run the backend with its demo data (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-backend#66) and sign in as one of the demo people.

## Home screen

My week is the page everyone lands on after signing in (decision 0038, "screens for each permission level"; #63). Under the week, it shows only the sections the person's work calls for, in this order:

| Section | Who sees it | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Needs your attention** | people with `time_off.review`, `announcement.create`, or `schedule.edit` and a home department | tiles: **Time off to review** (how many requests wait for this person, "200+" past one page, like the menu's count), and for people who plan the week **Open shifts this week** and **Open hours this week** in their own department; each tile is amber when there is work to do. Under the tiles, a link for each kind of work: **Review time off**, **Plan the department week**, and **Post an announcement** (opens the posting form at once, [announcements](announcements.md)). Someone who only posts gets the link without tiles, and nothing is asked of the server |
| **My time off** | people with `time_off.request_self` | up to three requests, soonest first: the ones still waiting for a decision (amber edge) and approved time off that has not ended; denied, cancelled, and past ones stay on the Time off page. Each shows its days and its status in words, then **All my time off** links to the Time off page. "No time off coming up." when there is none |
| **Announcements** | everyone | the newest three ([announcements](announcements.md)) |

- **Why on My week, not a page of its own:** employees open the application to see their week, so the week stays at the top on a phone; managers still find what needs them one scroll down, without a separate dashboard to learn.
- **This week only:** the attention tiles always count the current week, even while the person browses other weeks above them; the department week has the full numbers for any week.
- **Only a convenience:** each section is shown only with its permission, and the API checks every request again (threat E8). An employee who somehow showed a hidden section would get nothing the API would not already give them.
- **Posting an announcement:** the link (#79) goes to `pages/announcements-manage.html#post`, which opens the form straight away.
- **One request for the waiting list:** the menu's count and the tile share it (`waitingRequests()` in `js/components/app-shell.js`).
- **Tests:** `tests/unit/home.test.js` (which requests are listed and in what order, which tiles each kind of person gets, the requests sent, the empty and error states).

## Notes
- **The calendar** (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#82, slice CAL2):
  - **Layout:** a week view like Google Calendar's, which people already know: the days across (Monday to Sunday), the 24 hours down the side with labels ("12 AM" to "11 PM") and a light line at every hour. Each hour is 3rem tall, so the whole day does not fit on screen: the calendar scrolls up and down inside its card (the day headers stay pinned at the top), and it opens scrolled to the hour of the week's earliest shift start, or 6 AM when the week has no shifts (a night shift's morning piece does not count, or every week with a night shift would open at midnight). It replaced the horizontal timeline (one row per person) of workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#53, decided 2026-11-17 (decision 0038, revised).
  - **Blocks:** each shift is a block from its start to its end on the workplace's wall clock, the same clock as the hour labels, so a 9:00 to 17:00 shift always covers 9:00 to 17:00 of its day. A block shows the person's name (bold, up to two lines) and the time: "9 AM - 5 PM" on most blocks, "9a-5p" on narrow ones (a CSS container query on the block's own width), then a "+1" when it ends the next day and the event and notes icons; these move under the time when the line is too narrow. Blocks shorter than about 50 minutes put the name and time on one line. Pointing at a block shows who and the whole time as a tooltip ("Ana Diaz, 10 PM - 6 AM (ends the next day)"); screen readers hear "Ana Diaz, Monday, October 5, 10:00 PM - 6:00 AM (ends the next day)". Clicking opens the details popup.
  - **Screen readers:** each day is a list labelled with its full date ("Monday, October 5 (today)") holding that day's shifts in time order; the visible day headers and hour labels are skipped, since every block's description already says the day and the time.
  - **Overlaps:** shifts that overlap on the same day sit side by side, each narrower, as in Google Calendar. Overlapping shifts, and every shift linked to them through a chain of overlaps, form a group that shares the day's width in as many columns as it needs; a shift that overlaps nothing keeps the whole width. At the same start the longer shift gets the left column. Very narrow blocks (three or more side by side on a small laptop) use slightly smaller text so a first name still fits.
  - **Overnight shifts:** drawn in two pieces, from the start to midnight and from midnight to the end on the next day, each cut square with a dashed edge where it continues. The evening piece shows "10 PM - 6 AM" and "+1"; the morning piece shows "until 6 AM". The shift belongs to the day it starts, so only the evening piece is a button (in the Tab order, with the description and, in edit mode, the select box); the morning piece is hidden from screen readers but still opens the details when clicked.
  - **Edges of the week:** a Sunday night shift is drawn to Sunday midnight with a dashed bottom edge, and its tooltip and description add "(continues into next week)". A shift is listed in the week it starts, so the Monday morning part of last Sunday's night shift is not drawn on this week's Monday.
  - **The night the clocks fall back:** a 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM shift lasts 9 hours but is drawn over the 8 hours of the clock face (the details popup and the tiles count the real 9).
  - **Person colours:** each person keeps the same colour on every visit and every computer: it is chosen from their employee ID with a simple string hash (FNV-1a) out of a fixed palette of 12 colours (green, blue, purple, red, orange, teal, pink, olive, indigo, brown, cyan, slate). Nothing is stored. A block is a light tint of the colour with a strong left edge in it and dark text: every tint has at least 13:1 contrast with the text (10:1 on the darker tint under the mouse), and every edge at least 4.3:1 against its tint and 5:1 against white (WCAG 2.2 AA). Two people can share a colour, so the colour is never the only signal: the name is always on the block. Each block also carries `data-person` (the employee ID) for the people menu and its filters (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#83).
  - **Department picker:** a labelled list ("Department") in the heading with the departments from `GET /api/departments` (the person's own company only), the one shown selected. Choosing another loads the same week for that department in place, like the week links: the address changes to `&department=<id>` with `history.pushState` (so Back returns to the department before), and the heading, tiles, calendar, and sections follow. It is hidden when the company has only one department. For a department the page cannot show (see "Which department" below), it says "Choose a department" instead of pretending another one is shown.
  - **People menu** (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#83, slice CAL3; decided 2026-11-17): a card on the left of the calendar (laptops only, like the calendar; the phone drawer is slice CAL4) titled **People**, listing everyone with a shift in the week, once each:
    - **Sections** by the person's roles in the shift's department, in this order: Owners, Administrators, Managers, the company's own roles (by name, under the role's own name), Employees, and Other for anyone without a role. Each person is under the first of their roles in that order. Empty sections are left out.
    - **Each name** has the person's colour swatch and their **hours** for the week ("32 h", each shift's real length, overnight shifts included; screen readers hear "32 hours"), as the timeline had at the end of each row. The name is a toggle button (`aria-pressed`): clicking it shows or hides that person's shifts on the calendar. Switched off, the swatch is empty and the name struck through, so it is not told by colour alone.
    - **Each section** has a **Hide all** / **Show all** button (screen readers hear "Hide all Managers").
    - **Search names:** keeps the people whose name holds every word typed, in any order, ignoring capitals: "ana di" finds Ana Diaz but not Ana Park. The list narrows as well as the calendar. The search and the toggles combine: a shift shows only if its person matches the search **and** is switched on.
    - **The count** under the search ("2 of 3 people shown") is a polite live region, so screen readers hear it after each change. **Clear filters** shows everyone again and empties the search.
    - **The tiles** count only the shifts shown (open shifts always, since they belong to nobody), and while someone is hidden a line under them says "Filter on: these numbers count 2 of 3 people, and every open shift."
    - **Hidden, not removed:** a hidden person's blocks stay in the page (out of sight and out of the accessibility tree), so edit mode keeps its select boxes; the blocks still shown share each day's width again, so a shift that only overlapped hidden ones takes the whole column.
    - **Kept while the page is open:** the choices stay when another week or department loads (by employee ID), and are not saved between visits. The search runs in the browser over the shifts the page already has; it sends nothing.
    - **Tests:** `tests/unit/people-menu.test.js` (the sections and their order, the hours, the search, the toggles and section buttons, Clear filters, names as text, and the calendar's columns shared again); `tests/unit/pages.test.js` (the menu on the page, the tiles and their line, the choices kept across weeks); `tests/integration/calendar.spec.js` (in Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit behind the real policy: the menu beside the calendar, a hidden person's blocks gone and the others full width, the search, Clear filters, and no menu on a phone).
  - **Width:** laptop first, from 64rem; on wide desktop screens (90rem and up) the page uses most of the window, so side by side blocks get more room. Phones and tablets keep the day cards (the phone layout of the calendar is slice CAL4, workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#84).
  - **Content Security Policy:** block positions are CSS custom properties set through the DOM (`--block-start`, `--block-length`, `--block-column`, `--block-columns`), never `style` attributes, which the policy refuses ([nginx](../architecture/nginx.md)). The colour is a class name made from a number, never text from the server.
- **Overnight shifts** (for example 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM): a shift is listed on the day it **starts**, and every screen adds a small **+1 day** marker after its time (the calendar shortens it to **+1**) (screen readers hear "ends the next day"); the details popup also says when it ends ("Ends: Tuesday, October 6, 6:00 AM"). In the add and change form, an end time at or before the start time means the shift ends the next day (`shiftMoments` in `js/core/time.js`). This rule is never ambiguous because a shift is at most 12 hours long: an end later than the start can only be the same day, and an end earlier than the start can only be the next day. A line under the times shows the result as it will be saved ("Ends the next day, Tuesday, October 6 at 6:00 AM. 8 hours."), and turns amber with the reason when the shift has no length or is longer than the 12-hour limit (the server refuses those anyway). Daylight saving is included: 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM on the night the clocks fall back is 9 hours.
- **Time zones:** each shift carries its `timezone`; the page formats times in that zone with `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, not in the viewer's own zone (`js/core/time.js`). A shift belongs to the day it starts in its workplace zone, so a 22:00 shift stays on its own day even though it is already the next day in UTC.
- **Daylight saving:** when a wall-clock time is turned into UTC (`zonedTimeToUtc`, for creating shifts), a time that never happens because clocks spring forward (2:30 on the March change in the US) moves forward by the jump (3:30), and a time that happens twice because clocks fall back (1:30 on the November change) means the first one. These are the "compatible" rules of JavaScript's Temporal API.
- **Addresses:** both pages read `?week=YYYY-MM-DD` (the department week also `&department=<id>`), so a week can be bookmarked or shared. Any day of the week works; without a week, or with a day that does not exist (for example `2026-99-99`), the page shows the current week. Previous and next week links keep the department. **Back to current week** stays visible so the navigation does not move; it is disabled while showing the current week, and otherwise returns directly to the week containing today while keeping the department. A plain click on an enabled week link loads the other week in place (the address changes, Back returns to the week before), so the tabs and the name stay put; while it loads, the old week dims slightly instead of disappearing.
- **Which department:** the department week always shows one department, named in the heading, so shifts of different departments are never mixed. Without `&department=` it shows the person's own department, taken from the loaded session (if the session names none, the company's first department). The heading comes from the departments list, so a week with no shifts is still named. The address only chooses what to look at; the API decides what the person may see. A department that is not in the company's departments list (another company's ID, a department that no longer exists, a mistyped address, or an empty `&department=`) is not loaded at all: the page says "This department is unavailable. It may not exist, or you may not have access to it." and, when the address chose the department, offers **Show my department** for the same week (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#47). Every such ID gets the same words, so the page never confirms that a department exists in another company. (The shifts API does not answer 404 here: it filters the list to what the person may see, so another company's department would otherwise look like an empty week.)
- **Edit buttons** appear only when the session's permissions include `schedule.edit`; this is a convenience, the API checks every change.
- **Shifts stay short on the week screens:** each shows its time (and, in the department week, who works it), plus a small marker when it has an event (a star) or notes (a page): with the words "Event" and "Notes" on the day cards, icons only on the calendar's blocks (the words stay for screen readers). Clicking or tapping a shift, or pressing Enter on it, opens a popup with the rest: the day, who, role, department, event, and notes. The popup closes with its Close button or the Escape key, and keyboard focus goes back to the shift (`js/components/shift-dialog.js`).
- **Details, notes, and event text** are shown with `textContent`, never as HTML, in the popup as everywhere else.
- A 409 answer (double booking, time off, a shift that has ended) is shown next to the shift in plain words: in the form or question about that shift, or in the selection bar for a batch. The server's own words are used ("This person already has a shift at that time."), as text. 422 answers (for example "A shift cannot end in the past." or "This person cannot be assigned to this shift.") are shown the same way, and the form or bar stays as it was so nothing typed is lost.
- **Edit mode details** (workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-frontend#16):
  - **Only what changed is sent.** Changing a shift sends a `PATCH` with just the changed fields (so the audit entry lists exactly those), and an `assign` when the person changed. Emptying a text field sends `null`. These are two requests (the backend changes the person only through `assign`), so if the `PATCH` is saved and the `assign` is refused, the form says "The times and details were saved, but who works the shift was not changed." with the reason, the week behind it loads again, and trying again sends only the `assign`.
  - **One change at a time.** While a change is being sent, the form or question cannot be left (**Go back** is off and Escape is held back) and its buttons are off, and the selection bar's buttons are off until the week has loaded again. Closing the popup at that moment would look like "cancelled" while the server might still save the change. If a browser closes it anyway (Escape pressed twice in a row, which a page may not hold back again), the page still waits for the answer and reloads the week after a saved change.
  - **No answer is not "nothing saved".** When the server cannot be reached, the change may have been saved before the connection failed, so the message says "The server could not be reached, so the change may or may not have been saved. Check the schedule before trying again."
  - **Adding on several days** sends one `POST /api/shifts/batch` with `action: "create"`, so either every day's shift is added or none is. One day sends `POST /api/shifts`.
  - **Times** are typed in the workplace's zone, named under the field. A new shift uses the zone of the week's shifts, else the company's (`currentSession().company.timezone`); the backend copies the department's zone onto the shift either way. An end at or before the start means the shift ends the next day.
  - **Who can be chosen:** the department's people when the server lists them (`GET /api/users`, after workforce-ops-app/workforce-ops-backend#54), plus everyone who worked a shift in the department in the shown week or the three weeks before. Until the people endpoint exists, someone who has never worked in the department cannot be picked yet. The server still checks that the chosen person may work the shift (422 otherwise).
  - **Ended and cancelled shifts** get no select box and no edit buttons; their details say "This shift has ended, so it can no longer be changed." The server refuses them anyway (409).
  - **Permissions:** the Edit week button exists only with `schedule.edit`. A manager whose scope covers only part of the company sees the button for every department, but the server answers 403 outside their scope, shown as "You do not have permission to do this." (threat E8).
  - **Security, in course terms:** every change is a `POST` or `PATCH` with the `X-CSRF-Token` header added by `js/api/client.js` (CSRF); shift IDs are put into addresses with `encodeURIComponent`, so an ID can never change which address is called; every name, role, and note is shown with `textContent` (XSS); the count confirmation is checked by the server, not only asked by the page (decision 0025, threat D3).
